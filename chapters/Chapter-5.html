Chapter 5 Cells: The Working Units of Life

5.1 Cells Are the Fundamental Units of Life

  • Cell theory:

    1. Cells are the fundamental units of life.
    2. All living organisms are composed of cells.
    3. All cells come from preexisting cells.
    4. Modern cells evolved from a common ancestor.

  • 3 important implications of cell theory:

    1. Studying cell biology is in some sense the same as studying life.
    2. Life is continuous.
    3. The origin of life on Earth was marked by the origin of the first cells.

  • several important roles of membrane:

    • acts as a selectively permeable barrier:

      • prevent some substances from crossing in

      • permit other substances to enter & leave the cell

    • regulate transport across it \(\Rightarrow\) allows the cell to maintain a more or less constant internal environment

    • important in

      • communicating with adjacent cells

      • receiving signals from the environment

  • Everything inside the cell, except for the nucleus if the cell has one, is termed the cytoplasm.

  • The cytosol is the fluid cytoplasm not contained inside another compartment.

  • prokaryote: organisms in Archaea and Bacteria

    • does not have membrane-enclosed internal compartments
    • does not have a nucleus

  • eukaryote: protists 原生生物, plants, fungi, and animals

    • contain membrane-enclosed comaprtments called organelles

  • nucleus:

    • where most of the cell’s DNA is located

    • where gene expression begins

5.2 Prokaryotic Cells Are the Simplest Cells

  • nucleoid [病毒] 病毒核心; [细胞] 类核: region in the cell where the DNA is located

  • ribosomes 核糖体: complexes of RNA and proteins in the cytoplasm

  • cell wall

    • located outside the cell membrane

    • supports the cell

    • determines cells’ shape

  • cell wall of most bacteria, but not archaea:

    • contain peptidoglycan: a polymer of amino sugars that is linked at regular intervals to short peptides

  • in some bacteria, outer membrane: polysaccharide-rich phospholipid membrane

    • encloses the peptidoglycan layer

  • in some bacteria. capsule: a slimy layer composed mostly of polysaccharides

    • enclosing the cell wall

  • some bacteria have an internal membrane system: contains molecules needed for photosynthesis

  • some prokaryotes swim by using appendages called flagella

    • sometimes look like tiny corkscrews

  • pill: structures made of protein

    • project from the surfaces of some types of bacterial cells

  • cytoskeleton: protein filaments

    • play roles in cell division, cell movement, and in maintaining the shapes of cells.

5.3 Eukaryotic Cells Contain Organelles

  • nucleus:

    • location of most of the cell’s DNA and the site of DNA replication

    • the site where gene transcription is turned on or off

    • nucleolus: a region within the nucleus

      • where ribosomes begin to be assembled from RNA and proteins

  • the nucleus is enclosed by nuclear envelope: an integrated structure composed of two membranes

  • chromosomes: long, thin threads

    • made up by chromatin: inside nucleus, DNA molecule + proteins

  • endomembrane system: interconnected system of membrane-enclosed compartments, includes:

    • cell membrane

    • nuclear envelope

    • endoplasmic reticulum 内质网

    • Golgi apparatus [细胞] 高尔基氏体; 内网器

    • lysosomes 溶酶体

  • vesicles 囊: membrane-surrounded droplets

    • shuttle substances between the various components of the endomembrane system

  • endoplasmic reticulum (ER) 内质网: networks of interconnected membranes branching throughout the cytoplasm

    • forming tubes and flattened sacs

  • rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) 带有核糖体的内质网: many ribosomes attached to the outer surface of the membrane, giving it a “rough” appearance in electron microscopy

    • receives into its lumen 内腔 (管状器官内的空腔) certain newly synthesized proteins

      • segregating them away from the cytoplasm

      • participates in transporting these proteins to other locations in the cell

    • proteins can be

      • chemically modified to alter their functions

      • “tag” them for delivery to specific cellular destinations

    • proteins are sent to destinations elsewhere in the cell enclosed within vesicles that pinch off from the RER

    • most membrane-bound proteins are made in the RER

    • glycoproteins 糖蛋白: some proteins become covalently linked to carbohydrate groups in the RER

  • smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER) 平滑内质网: lacks ribosomes and is more tubular (and less like fattened sacs) than the RER, but shows continuity with portions of the RER

    • responsible for the chemical modification of small molecules taken in by the cell that may be toxic to the cell

      • make the targeted molecules more polar, so they are more water soluble and easily removed

    • is the site for glycogen degradation in animal cells

    • where lipids and steroids are synthesized, as well as some polysaccharides 多糖 in plant cells

    • store calcium ions, released trigger numerous cell responses

  • Golgi apparatus 内网器, consists of two components: flattened membranous sacs called cisternae 潴泡 (singular cisterna) that are piled up like saucers, and small membrane-enclosed vesicles

    • receives protein-containing vesicles from the RER

    • modifies, concentrates, packages, and sorts proteins. before they are sent to their cellular or extracellular destinations

    • adds carbohydrates to proteins

    • modifies other carbohydrates that were attached to proteins in the RER

    • where some polysaccharides for the plant cell wall are synthesized

    • cuts certain large precursor proteins transferred from the RER into smaller, functional fragments

  • 3 functionally distinct regions (faces) of Golgi apparatus:

    1. The cis face lies nearest to the nucleus or a patch of RER
    2. The trans face lies closest to the cell membrane
    3. The medial face lies in between

  • primary lysosomes: originate from the Golgi apoparatus

  • contain digestive enzymes

  • the sites where macromolecules (protein, polysaccharides, nucleic acids, lipids) are hydrolyzed into their monomers

  • phagocytosis 吞噬作用: a process of food, other cells, or foreign objects enter the cell

  • secondary lysosome [细胞][免疫] 次级溶酶体: phagosome 吞噬体 fuses with a primary lysosome to form a ~

    • where digestion occurs

    • there reactions are enhanced by the acidity of the lysosome’s interior

  • exocytosis (细胞生物学) 胞吐作用:

    • the “used” secondary lysosome containing undigested particles

    • moves to the cell membrane

    • fuses with it, and releases the undigested contents to the environment

  • autophagy (生物学) 自噬: programmed destruction of cell components

    • some cell components are continually being broken down and replaced by new ones

  • lysosomes 溶酶体:

    • where the cell breaks down its own materials

    • with the proper signal, lysosomes can engulf entire organelles, hydrolyzing their constituents

  • lysosomal storage diseases: caused by the failure of lysosomes to digest specific cellular components

    • invariably harmful or fatal

    • e.g. Tay-Sachs disease 黑蒙性家族性痴愚; 泰-萨二氏病

      • a particular lipid called a ganglioside [生化] 神经节苷脂 is not broken down in the lysosomes

      • instead accumulates in brain cells

      • a baby starts exhibiting neurological symptoms and becomes blind, deaf, and unable to swallow after 6 months of age

      • death occurs before age 4

  • plant cells do not appear to contain lysosomes, but the vacuole [细胞] 液泡 of a plant cell may function in an equivalent capacity because it, like lysosomes, hydrolyzes macromolecules

  • energy is harvested from

    • fuel molecules in the mitochondria 线粒体 (mitochondrion 的复数)

      • found in all eukaryotic cells

    • sunlight in the chloroplasts 叶绿体 of plant cells\

  • energy in biological systems is stored and transferred in the form of covalent bonds in organic molecules

  • In eukaryotic cells, breakdown of energy-rich molecules sucha s glucose begins in the cytoplasm.

  • mitochondria:

    • harvests the chemical-bond energy of those molecules in a form that he cell can use

    • namely the energy-rich molecule ATP (adenosine triphosphate 腺苷三磷酸)

    • some can reproduce and divide independently of the central nucleus

    • Cells that are active in movement and growth require the most chemical-bond energy and tend to have the most mitochondria.

  • cellular respiration

    • production of ATP in the mitochondria

    • using fuel molecules and molecular oxygen (\(\ce{O2}\))
