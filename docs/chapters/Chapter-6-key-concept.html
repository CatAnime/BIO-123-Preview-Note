Chapter 6: Cell Membranes

Key Concepts

  1. Biological membranes are lipid-protein bilayers.

  2. The cell membrane is important in cell adhesion and recognition.

  3. Substances can cross membranes by passive processes.

  4. Active transport across membranes requires energy.

  5. Large molecules enter and leave a cell through vesicles.

6.1 Biological Membranes Are Lipid-Protein Bilayers

Membrane Composition & Structure

  • phospholipid bilayer: hydrophilic heads face outward, hydrophobic tails face inward.

  • fluid mosaic model: proteins float in or on the lipid bilayer.

Lipid Properties

  • Phospholipids vary in chain length, saturation, and polar groups.

  • Cholesterol modulates membrane fluidity and stability.

  • Fluidity affected by:

    • Lipid composition (saturated vs unsaturated fatty acids)

      • saturated = \(\Downarrow\) fluidity

      • unsaturated = \(\Uparrow\) fluidity

    • Temperature

      • \(\Downarrow\) T = \(\Downarrow\) fluidity

      • \(\Uparrow\) T = \(\Uparrow\) fluidity

Types of Membrane Proteins

  • integral proteins: embedded in bilayer, have hydrophilic and hydrophobic domains

  • peripheral proteins: bound to membrane surface, no hydrophobic domains

  • anchored proteins: covalently attached to lipid groups in the bilayer

Protein Distribution & Mobility

  • Proteins are asymmetrically 不对称地 distributed.

  • Cell fusion experiments show proteins can diffuse laterally.

  • Cytoskeleton can restrict protein movement.

Membrane Dynamics

  • Membranes are constantly forming, fusing, and recycling via vesicles.

  • Different organelles have different membrane compositions.

Membrane Carbohydrates

  • Glycolipids, glycoproteins, and proteoglycans are involved in recognition and adhesion.

  • Carbohydrate structures provide diverse recognition signals.

6.2 The Cell Membrane Is Important in Cell Adhesion and Recognition

Cell Recognition & Adhesion

  • Mediated by membrane proteins and carbohydrates.

  • Sponge cell experiments show species-specific recognition.

Adhesion Molecules

  • Proteoglycans with sulfated polysaccharides mediate recognition and binding.

  • Adhesion can be homotypic (same molecule) or heterotypic (different molecules).

Types of Cell Junctions (Animals)

  • tight junction 紧密连接: seal intercellular spaces

  • desmosome 细胞桥粒: provide mechanical strength

  • gap junction 间隙连接: allow communication via channels

Cell-Extracellular Matrix Adhesion

  • integrins 整联蛋白 connect ECM to cytoskeleton and enable cell movement

6.3 Substances Can Cross Membranes by Passive Processes

Diffusion

  • Net movememnt from high to low concentration

  • rate depends on

    • molecular size / mass (\(\mathrm{m}\))

    • temperature (\(\mathrm{T}\))

    • solution density (\(\rho\))

    • concentration gradient (\(\frac{\mathrm{d}c}{\mathrm{d}x}\))

    • surface area and distance (\(\mathrm{A}\) and \(\mathrm{d}\))

Simple Diffusion

  • Small, hydrophobic molecules pass directly through the bilayer.

  • Polar/charged molecules diffuse poorly.

Osmosis

  • Water moves toward higher solute concentration.

  • Terms:

    • isotonic 等渗的: equal solute concentrations

    • hypotonic 低渗的: lower solute outside \(\Longrightarrow\) water enters cell

    • hypertonic 高渗的: higher solute outside \(\Longrightarrow\) water leaves cell

  • Plant cells use turgor pressure 渗透压 to maintain structure.

    • 渗透压: 植物细胞内液体由于渗入作用而产生的实际压力, 与具有相同浓度的液体在理论上可能产生的最大压力相对比。

Facilitated Diffusion

  • channel proteins 离子通道蛋白 form hydrophilic pores

    • 离子通道蛋白: 一种成孔蛋白, 通过允许特定类型的离子穿过通道来帮助细胞建立和控制质膜间的微弱电压差

    • e.g., ion channels, aquaporins 水通道蛋白, 水孔蛋白

  • carrier proteins 载体蛋白 bind substances and change shape

    • 载体蛋白: 一种功能是通过生物膜运输小分子(或其他蛋白质)的蛋白质

    • e.g., glucose transporter

6.4 Active Transport across Membranes Requires Energy

General Features

  • moves substances against concentration / electrical gradients

  • requries energy (usually ATP)

Types of Transport Proteins

  • uniporter 单向传递体: moves one substance one way

  • symporter 协同载体: moves two substances same direction

  • antiporter 反向转运体: moves two substances opposite directions

Primary Active Transport

  • direct use of ATP

  • e.g., \(\ce{Na+ - K+}\) pump

    • 3 \(\ce{Na+}\) out, 2 \(\ce{K+}\) in

    • \(\ce{ATP -> ADP + P_i}\)

Secondary Active Transport (Co-trnasport)

  • Uses energy from ion gradient (established by primary transport)

  • e.g., \(\ce{Na+ - glucose}\) symporter in intestinal cells 肠道细胞

  • applied in oral rehydration therapy (ORT) 口服补液疗法

6.5 Large Molecules Enter and Leave a Cell through Vesicles

Endocytosis (into the cell)

  • phagocytosis 吞噬作用 (“cell eating”): large particles or cells

  • pinocytosis 胞饮作用 (“cell drinking”): fluids and dissolved substances

  • receptor-mediated endocytosis 受体介导的内吞作用: specific uptake via receptor proteins in coated pits

    • 一种细胞摄取特定物质的过程, 通过特定的受体与物质结合, 然后将受体与物质一起内化到细胞内部

    • e.g., LDL cholesterol 低密度脂蛋白胆固醇 uptake

Exocytosis (out of the cell)

  • vesicles fuse with cell membrane to release contents

  • e.g., neurotransmitter release 神经递质释放

    • 神经元通过释放神经递质来传递信号的过程

  • e.g., sweat secretion

Investigating Life - Aquaporins 水孔蛋白

  • water channels that allow rapid, selective water movement

  • important in kidneys, plant roots, and vacuoles

  • can be regulated (gated) in response to environmental conditions

  • potential applications: water purification, desalination 海水淡化, cosmetics
